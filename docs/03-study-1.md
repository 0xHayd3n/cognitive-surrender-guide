## The contrast

The main laboratory sample contains 359 participants: 121 assigned to answer without AI, 238 assigned optional AI access. The AI-available condition is deliberately oversampled two to one. Recommendation quality varies across items within AI-available participants. An additional 81-person online sample is described as a robustness sample excluded from the main Study 1 results but included later in the synthesis. (pp. 21–22; p. 41 footnote.)

The strongest comparison here is the randomized assignment of AI availability combined with engineered recommendation quality. The experiment asks whether access changes final responses differently when advice is correct or wrong. It does not ask whether AI users are generally smarter, whether GPT-4o naturally gets these items right, or whether people should never delegate reasoning.

## Consultation and adoption

The authors report consultation on 54.4% of AI-Accurate trials and 52.8% of AI-Faulty trials. These similar use rates help distinguish availability from actual use: nearly half the available trials do not involve consultation. Among consulted trials, people follow correct recommendations 92.7% of the time, 95% CI [90.2,95.0], and faulty recommendations 79.8%, 95% CI [75.9,83.4]. Faulty-advice override is consequently 20.2%, versus 7.3% for correct advice. The reported override contrast OR is 4.19, 95% CI [2.03,8.65]. (p. 22; Figure 2 p. 23.)

Two conclusions coexist. People discriminate somewhat between good and bad advice, since they reject bad advice more often. Yet conditional acceptance of the bad advice remains high. A description that says participants never checked would overstate the behavioral evidence.

## Confidence

Global confidence is higher with AI access: mean 77.0, 95% CI [74.4,79.6], versus 65.3, 95% CI [61.0,69.6], without access, a difference of 11.7 points. The reported Hedges' g is 0.54, 95% CI [0.32,0.77]. Within the AI-access condition, the number of faulty trials does not significantly predict lower confidence (p=.202). (p. 25.)

Because confidence is measured once after the task, this is evidence of elevated overall confidence despite exposure to faulty AI. It is not an item-level demonstration that every wrong recommendation immediately increases confidence. Study 3 supplies the item-level measure.

## Individual differences

Higher Trust in AI predicts greater use and more following of faulty advice; greater fluid intelligence and NFC are associated with better accuracy on faulty trials. But several secondary results are weaker or null: fluid intelligence does not significantly predict lower use (p=.062), NFC does not reliably predict adoption conditional on consultation (p=.410), and NFCC does not moderate accuracy or use even though it is associated with adoption. Avoid flattening this into a rule that every analytical trait protects every outcome. (pp. 25–26, Figure 4.)

## What this supports

Advice quality strongly changes final answer accuracy in this task, and consultation often leads to adoption even when the advice is faulty. Calling that pattern cognitive surrender is the authors' interpretation. A distinctive third system, the absence of deliberation on each trial, and enduring loss of reasoning ability are broader claims that these response data do not independently establish. The next experiment tests whether the behavior changes under time pressure.
